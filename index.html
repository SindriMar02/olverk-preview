Pilsner, 5,2%

Eilífur

Eilífur in Hveragerði is the people’s geyser, and it erupts fairly regularly with a little help. Golden, with floral fruit drifting through the malt and a clean balance of sweet and bitter. Something for everyone.

Named after Eilífur, Hveragerði

Skyr sour, 4,2%

Grænihver

Grænihver is named for the pale green water you see when you look straight down into it. This light sour is sweet and sour at once, with a little of Hveragerði’s most famous ice lolly and a splash of skyr in the recipe.

Named after Grænihver, Hveravellir

Léttöl, dark mild, 2,0%

Litlihver

Litlihver simmers quietly on the bed of the Brúará river. A dark, full-flavoured light beer in the dark mild style, with roasted caramel and a hint of vanilla. A brewer’s beer for the one who drives home.

Named after Litlihver, Brúará in Biskupstungur

Red ale, 5,5%

Rauðihver

Rauðihver at Hveravellir is a big, clay-red spring that splashes now and then, just to remind you it is there. A full, layered red ale where soft caramel meets lightly toasted malt.

Named after Rauðihver, Hveravellir

IPA, 6,5%

Rótandi

Rótandi is a young spring. Coarse sand churns on its floor and turns the water beautifully cloudy, just like this bright IPA. Citrus and tropical fruit on the nose, hops playing against a light body.

Named after Rótandi, Hveravellir

Brewed with heat from the ground.

Hveragerði sits on the hot ground of the Hengill area. Ölverk brews in small batches on site, in plain view at the back of the restaurant, and the brewery runs on that geothermal energy. Every can carries the same line: þessi bjór er bruggaður með jarðhita í Hveragerði.

A beer for every season.

Five seasonals, each named after a geyser or a crater. From Þorri in January to Grýla at Christmas.

  1. Þorri

    Sóði

    Belgian pale ale, 7,0%

    Named after a lively geyser in Haukadalur that spat filthy water when it first erupted, around 1940. Cloudy too, but brewed with spotless water.

  2. Easter

    Kátur

    Pastry cream ale, 5,0%

    Kátur on Hrafntinnusker erupts almost without pause, a bottomless source of joy. Madagascan cacao husk and coconut give it a fruity chocolate note.

  3. Summer

    Cuexcomate

    Table saison, 4,9%

    A dormant geyser in Mexico, ringed by prickly pear. A summer saison with melon and red berries.

  4. Autumn

    Hrekkur

    Festbier, 6,1%

    An explosion crater born in the 1961 Askja eruption, with no warning at all. Brewed in the German festival style: full of flavour, still light.

  5. Christmas

    Grýla

    Winter ale, 5,2%

    The geyser Grýla in Hveragerði and the troll of the same name have both mellowed with age. Malt, cinnamon, pine and orange. Let her warm up a little before drinking.

The beer festival in the greenhouse.

Every autumn, the old plant-breeding greenhouse behind the brewpub turns into a festival hall for brewers from around Iceland. The 2026 festival ran 2-3 October. Follow along on Instagram for next year’s dates.
